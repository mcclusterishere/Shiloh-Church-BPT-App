Before we begin

What this is

In about an hour, your church has its own app — events, giving, prayer, live worship, all under your own name. This studio walks you through it one small step at a time, and no question here is technical.

Worth gathering first (none of it is required to start):

  • Your logo as an image file (JPG or PNG)
  • A few photos of your congregation for later
  • Your service times and office info — address, phone, email
  • Your giving handles — Givelify, Cash App, PayPal, Zelle, whatever you use

Two honest promises. First: you can stop anytime — your answers stay on this computer, and this page picks up right where you left off. Second: this studio generates files and shows you exactly where to put them. It never touches your app, your account, or the internet by itself.

Step 2

Where each fact shows up

  • Address — the front door, the visitor FAQ, every event's location, and the "get directions" button.
  • Phone & email — tap-to-call and tap-to-email buttons throughout.
  • Website & socials — linked from the About screen and the footer.
  • Prayer line — its own card on the Prayer screen. Skip it if you don't have one; the card simply never appears.
  • Service times — the front door's schedule, and in the next steps they seed your calendar and your live-stream window.
  • Kids & parking notes — the "New Here" screen, where first-time visitors look before they visit.

Anything you leave blank stays quietly hidden in the app — blank is always a supported answer.

Step 3

The faces of the church

Your pastor's name appears on the front door and the About screen; the short greeting becomes the opening words of your "Who we are" section — the first thing a visitor reads.

Staff and leaders appear as cards on the About screen, in the order you add them. Bios are optional — a name and a title is a perfectly good card.

Photos come later: after the files are in place, you'll drop portrait images into assets/media/ and add each one's filename in the app's admin — or just edit the file again. No photo means a clean, initialed card, never a broken image.

Step 4

Ministries and spaces

Each checked ministry becomes a card on the Ministries screen with an "I'm interested" button that sends the person's name to your admin inbox. The one-line mission is what shows on the card — you can grow it into a full description later.

Bookable spaces are different: they appear both as facilities members can reserve and on the public "Rent our space" screen for outside congregations and community groups. Rates stay blank until your office sets them — the app shows "Ask about rates" and never takes a payment.

Rename anything. "Women's Ministry" can be "Sisters of Grace" — the list is a head start, not a rule.

Step 5

How giving works here

The design has one non-negotiable, written into the giving file itself: "the app only LINKS out to these — it never processes or moves money itself."

That means no fees, no card forms, no compliance burden, and nothing to breach. Each method you check becomes a card on the Give screen that opens the real Givelify / Cash App / PayPal / Zelle page — the same handles your congregation already uses.

"By mail" and "in person" get cards too. For many members those are still the main way, and the app honors that.

    Step 9
    
        
    Where your app's memory lives
    
        
    Demo mode needs nothing: every RSVP and prayer request lives in the browser it was typed on. Perfect for a pilot; one browser only, so two staff phones won't see each other's inbox.
    
        
    Supabase is a real shared database with a generous free tier. If you choose it, here is the whole journey:
    
        
      
          
    1. Create an account at supabase.com — email and a password.
      2. 
          
    2. New project — give it your church's name, set a strong database password (save it somewhere safe; you rarely need it again), pick the region nearest you.
      3. 
          
    3. Find your two values. In your project's dashboard, open the settings (the gear icon in the left sidebar) and choose API. The Project URL is at the top — it looks like https://abcdefgh.supabase.co. Below it, under "Project API keys," copy the one labeled anon public. Never copy the one labeled service_role — that one is secret and belongs nowhere near a website.
      4. 
          
    4. Paste both into the fields on the right.
      5. 
          
    5. Set up the tables. Press "Copy the database setup" on the right, then in the Supabase dashboard open SQL Editor (left sidebar), paste, and press Run. It creates every table with row-level security already on.
      6. 
          
    6. Test it with the button on the right — it asks your project a harmless question and tells you in plain words what came back.
      7. 
        
    
        
    Privacy, stated plainly: the URL and key you paste here are saved only inside this page's draft, in your browser's local storage on this computer — nowhere else. The anon key is designed to be public; the safety comes from the row-level security the setup script turns on.
    
      
    

      
    
        
    Step 10
    
        
    Going live on Sundays
    
        
    The app's Watch screen has a built-in player that accepts any embed link. Three honest routes, from the app's own go-live runbook (docs/GO-LIVE.md):
    
        
      
          
    • YouTube embed — stream to YouTube (free) and the app plays it inside your own design. Most churches pair it with Restream (from about $19–49/month) to reach Facebook at the same time.
      • 
          
    • Your own box — $0/month: a staff phone becomes the camera and a small church-owned computer does the fan-out. It's real and documented, but it's an afternoon project for later, not tonight.
      • 
          
    • Skip for now — the Watch screen shows your schedule and a friendly note instead of a player. Nothing breaks.
      • 
        
    
        
    Whatever you pick today is one line in one file to change later.
    
      
    

      
    
        
    Step 12 · Where the files go
    
        
    The placement tutorial
    
        
    Your files download to this computer. Here's how they get into your app, using only the GitHub website:
    
        
      
          
    1. Open your fork of the app on github.com (the copy under your account).
      2. 
          
    2. Click into the data folder.
      3. 
          
    3. Press Add file → Upload files (top right of the file list).
      4. 
          
    4. Drag in every JSON file you downloaded on the right. GitHub will say they replace the existing ones — that's exactly right.
      5. 
          
    5. At the bottom, press the green Commit changes button.
      6. 
        
    
        
    Icons (if you generated them in step 8): do the same dance once more — go back to the repository's front page, open assets, then icons, Upload files, drag in the five PNGs, Commit changes.
    
        
    Manifest colors and name: open manifest.webmanifest at the repository's front page, press the pencil (Edit), and update the lines shown on the right. Commit.
    
        
    Then turn the website on: in your repository go to Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions. The next change you commit publishes the app. The full launch runbook — custom domain and all — is docs/GO-LIVE.md, step 1.
    
        
    The deep, complete fork checklist (hardcoded strings, photo swaps, every corner) lives in docs/TEMPLATE.md. This studio covers the heart of it; that document covers all of it.
